What Is SCUBA Diving?

SCUBA stands for Self-Contained Underwater Breathing Apparatus. It lets you carry your own air supply so you can explore underwater far longer than a single breath-hold allows — swimming alongside coral reefs, wrecks, and marine life most people only ever see in photos. You don't need to be an elite athlete or a strong swimmer. You just need proper training, a bit of comfort in the water, and a healthy respect for the rules that keep diving safe.

Core Safety Principles

  • Always dive with a certified buddy — never dive alone
  • Never hold your breath underwater; breathe continuously and naturally
  • Ascend slowly and make a 3-minute safety stop at 5m/15ft before surfacing
  • Plan your dive and dive your plan — stay within your training and no-decompression limits
  • Equalize your ears early and often as you descend

Buoyancy Control

Good buoyancy is what separates a nervous beginner from a relaxed, confident diver. Your BCD (buoyancy control device) and the right amount of weight get you close to neutral buoyancy, while your breathing does the fine-tuning — inhale to rise slightly, exhale to sink slightly. Master this and you'll use less air, protect the reef and any wrecks you explore, and feel like you're floating rather than swimming.

Tip: Most new divers are overweighted. Proper weighting is one of the fastest ways to improve buoyancy — it's a core focus of the Peak Performance Buoyancy specialty.

Underwater Communication

Since you can't talk underwater, divers use a simple set of hand signals to stay in sync.

Everyday Signals

  • OK: Thumb and finger circle, or arm circle overhead
  • Something's Wrong: Flat hand rocked side to side
  • Ascend / Descend: Thumb up or thumb down
  • Look at This: Point, then form a circle with thumb and finger at your eyes

Emergency Signals

  • Out of Air: Flat hand slicing across the throat
  • Low on Air: Fist tapping the chest
  • Stop: Open palm held out flat
  • Get My Buddy's Attention: Tap tank with a metal object, or bang/knock loudly

Skills You'll Learn in Open Water

  • Equipment setup and buddy safety checks
  • Buoyancy control and efficient fin techniques
  • Mask clearing and regulator recovery
  • Controlled emergency swimming ascent
  • Basic underwater navigation
